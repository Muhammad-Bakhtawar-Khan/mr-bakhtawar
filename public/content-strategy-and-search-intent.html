Content Strategy and Search Intent

A sustainable content strategy starts with real audience questions and the information you can reliably provide. Publishing fewer, more distinct pages is often more useful than scaling near-identical posts.

Build a topic inventory

List the pages you already have, their primary audience, purpose, supporting evidence and internal links. Identify overlap before drafting anything new. A page that serves a distinct task may deserve to remain separate even when it shares vocabulary with another page.

Map intent to a page type

People may want to learn a concept, compare options, complete a task or evaluate a service. Match the page format to that need: a checklist for implementation, a guide for learning, a project page for demonstrated work, and a contact page for enquiries.

Create a useful brief

State the intended reader, question, key points, facts that need verification, examples available, related internal pages and what the page should help the reader do next. Identify evidence gaps instead of filling them with assumptions.

Write with evidence and clarity

Use plain language, explain unfamiliar terms and provide concrete steps or examples. Separate verified experience from general advice. Do not invent client results, testimonials, qualifications, publication dates or statistics.

Maintain and consolidate carefully

Review pages for accuracy, outdated links and overlap. If consolidating URLs, choose the closest relevant destination, transfer genuinely useful information, update internal links and test redirects. Review Search Console data before irreversible removals when available.

About this guide: This is general educational guidance. Adapt recommendations to your site's actual stack, risk and audience, and verify changes before publishing.
