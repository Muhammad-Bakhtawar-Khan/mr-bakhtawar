A good project should never feel like a mystery. I’m Bakhtawar Khan, founder of MBK Global. Here’s the practical path we follow to turn your idea into a considered, useful digital result—with clear communication, agreed milestones and a proper handover.

MBK / PROJECT DELIVERY

From “I have an idea” to “It’s ready.”

Every project is different, so the exact deliverables and timeline are agreed before work begins. This four-step framework keeps expectations clear and gives you opportunities to review the direction along the way.

First · Discover

We understand your idea.

First, we discuss your goals, audience, references, features, deadline and budget. We identify the real problem to solve and define what success should look like.

  • Project brief and requirements
  • Goals, audience and priorities
01
02
Second · Plan

We shape the direction.

Next, we turn the brief into a practical plan. Depending on the project, this may include page structure, a visual direction, a feature list, milestones and a delivery schedule.

  • Scope and milestones agreed
  • Design direction or technical plan
Third · Build & review

We create, test and refine.

Then the design or development work begins. You can review agreed checkpoints, share feedback and help us refine the details before final delivery.

  • Build, integration and quality checks
  • Feedback and agreed revisions
03
04
Finally · Deliver

Your final project is handed over.

Finally, we review the agreed deliverables and hand over the finished work. Depending on the scope, this can include source files, deployment assistance, documentation and guidance for what comes next.

  • Final review against the brief
  • Handover and next-step guidance

THE FINISH LINE IS A HANDOVER

Built with purpose.
Delivered with clarity.

Our goal is not just to finish a task, but to give you a result you understand and can move forward with. The final scope, payment stages, revisions, third-party fees and ongoing support are agreed in writing before work starts—so there are no hidden assumptions.
